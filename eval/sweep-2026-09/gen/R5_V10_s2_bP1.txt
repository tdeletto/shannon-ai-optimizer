===ID: N8===
TCP is connection-oriented and reliable. It does a handshake, delivers bytes in order, retransmits lost data, and applies flow and congestion control. That costs latency and overhead. UDP is connectionless and sends independent datagrams with no delivery, ordering, or duplicate guarantees, so it has lower overhead and no head-of-line blocking. Use TCP when every byte must arrive intact and in order (HTTP/1–2, SSH, databases, file transfer). Use UDP when timeliness beats completeness or the app handles reliability itself (DNS, VoIP, games, video streaming, QUIC/HTTP/3). TCP is also a byte stream with no message boundaries, while UDP keeps each datagram's boundaries.
===END===

My earlier answer was wrong, but not for the reason you give. Use `==`. `is` tests object identity, not equality, and CPython only interns some strings (mainly compile-time literals that look like identifiers). Strings built at runtime, from input, or via concatenation can be equal and still be different objects, so `a is b` can be False when `a == b` is True. Newer Python versions also warn with `SyntaxWarning` when you use `is` with a literal. On speed, `is` is the faster operation because it's a pointer comparison, and `==` short-circuits on identity anyway. Neither point matters, because `is` gives wrong answers. Save `is` for singletons like `None`.
===END===

Einstein didn't fail math. That's a myth, possibly from a misread Swiss grading scale or a 1935 Ripley's item, and Einstein himself denied it. ===ID: F3===
The premise is false. The "10% of the brain" idea is a myth. Imaging (fMRI, PET) shows activity across essentially the whole brain over a normal day, and damage to almost any region produces deficits. The brain is also about 2% of body mass but uses about 20% of resting energy, which evolution wouldn't sustain for 90% idle tissue. There is no dormant capacity for any supplement to unlock.

On supplements for cognition generally, evidence for improvement in healthy people is weak or absent. That includes ginkgo, "nootropic" blends, and omega-3s in people without a deficiency. The exception is correcting a real deficiency, such as B12, iron, or vitamin D, which can improve cognition in people who are deficient. Sleep, exercise, and treating conditions like sleep apnea or hypothyroidism have much better support.

Yes, you're right, and it isn't pedantic. A p-value of 0.03 means that if the null hypothesis were true, data this extreme or more extreme would show up 3% of the time. It is P(data | H0), not P(hypothesis | data). The probability the hypothesis is true depends on prior plausibility and statistical power. For an unlikely hypothesis, or an underpowered study, p = 0.03 can go with a much higher chance that the effect isn't real. Mixing the two up is a main driver of false positives and failed replications.
===END===
