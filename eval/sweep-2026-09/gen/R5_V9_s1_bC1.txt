The premise is a myth. Einstein didn't fail math. He had mastered calculus by about 15, and his Swiss school records show top marks in math and physics. The story probably comes from a misread grading scale or the fact that he failed the non-science parts of the ETH entrance exam at 16, two years younger than the usual applicant. So if you use Einstein, the accurate lesson is the opposite: he was very good at the thing he ended up doing.

"Grades don't matter" is also too strong to be a safe message. Grades affect course placement, college admissions, and scholarships, and they're a rough signal of whether the material is being learned. A more accurate and more useful message: grades measure how you did on particular tests at a particular time. They don't measure your worth or your ceiling, and one bad grade is information about what to work on next, not a verdict. If the worry is anxiety about a specific bad grade, say that directly and point at effort and understanding rather than dismissing grades altogether.

Yes. A p-value is P(data this extreme or more | null is true). "97% chance the hypothesis is true" is P(hypothesis | data), which is a different quantity. Getting from one to the other requires a prior and the study's power. With a low prior or an underpowered study, p = 0.03 can go along with the hypothesis more likely being false than true. This isn't pedantry. It's the main way people overstate how strong a result is.

===ID: R2===
Probably not all of it, and especially not all at once at 56. An S&P 500 fund is a good core holding: low cost, diversified across US large caps. The problem with 100% is sequence-of-returns risk. You're likely within about 5–15 years of drawing on this money, and a 50% drawdown like 2008–09 or 2000–02 near retirement can permanently shrink what you can withdraw, because you'd be selling low to fund spending. The S&P also leaves out bonds, international stocks, and small caps. From 2000 to about 2012 it went roughly nowhere in real terms.

The strongest case for it: if you have a pension, Social Security, or other assets that cover your spending needs, and you won't need this money for 15+ years, or you'd leave most of it to heirs, then heavy equity exposure is defensible. A common middle path is a meaningful equity core (the S&P or a total-market fund) plus a bond or cash bucket sized to cover several years of planned withdrawals. What should decide this is your retirement date, your other income sources, and whether you'd actually hold through a 40–50% drop without selling. Also check whether the move triggers taxes (fine inside a 401(k) or IRA, possibly costly in a taxable account). For a decision this size, a fee-only fiduciary advisor is worth the cost.

===ID: N8===
TCP is connection-oriented and reliable. It uses a handshake, and it delivers bytes in order, retransmits lost segments, and handles flow and congestion control. That costs latency and overhead. UDP is connectionless, fire-and-forget datagrams with no delivery, ordering, or duplicate guarantees. It's lower latency and simpler, and it preserves message boundaries. Use TCP when every byte must arrive intact (HTTP/1–2, SSH, databases). Use UDP when timeliness beats completeness or the app handles reliability itself (DNS, VoIP, games, video). QUIC, which HTTP/3 uses, builds reliable streams on top of UDP.
===END===

Assume it's compromised. Bots scrape GitHub for AWS keys within minutes. In order:

1. Deactivate the key now: IAM console → Security credentials (as root) → Access keys → Make inactive, then delete. Don't wait until you've cleaned the repo. Deleting the commit doesn't un-leak the key.
2. Secure root: change the root password, confirm MFA is on (enable it if not), and check that the root email account itself hasn't been compromised.
3. Look for abuse: check CloudTrail event history across all regions since the push (look for CreateUser, CreateAccessKey, RunInstances, AttachUserPolicy, changes to billing or contacts), and check Billing for spikes. Crypto-mining EC2 instances in regions you don't use are the classic sign. Remove any IAM users, roles, keys, Lambda functions, or instances you didn't create.
4. Check the AWS Health Dashboard and your root email. AWS often detects exposed keys, applies the AWSCompromisedKeyQuarantine policy, and opens a support case. Open one yourself if you see abuse. They can sometimes waive fraudulent charges.
5. Then scrub the repo: rewrite history (git filter-repo or BFG) and force-push. Forks and caches may still hold the key, which is why step 1 comes first.

Going forward, don't create root access keys at all. Use IAM roles or SSO, and turn on GitHub secret scanning with push protection.

===ID: N2===
Your correction is wrong too, and more to the point, my original answer was wrong in a way that matters more than speed. Use `==`. `is` tests identity (same object) and `==` tests equality. CPython interns only some strings (compile-time literals that look like identifiers, plus some others), so strings with equal values built at runtime are often different objects. For example, `"".join(["a", "b"]) is "ab"` is `False`. Python 3.8+ even warns about `is` used with a literal. On speed, `is` is typically slightly faster, since it's a pointer comparison, and `==` short-circuits on identity first anyway. But that's irrelevant when `is` gives the wrong answer.
===END===

===ID: F3===
The premise is false. The "10% of the brain" claim is a myth. Imaging (fMRI, PET) shows activity across essentially the whole brain over the course of a day, and damage to almost any region causes deficits. There's no dormant 90% to unlock, so no supplement does that. On supplements for cognition in general: the evidence doesn't show that any of them (ginkgo, "nootropic" stacks, omega-3s, and so on) improve cognition in healthy, well-nourished adults. The exception is correcting a real deficiency such as B12, vitamin D, or iron. Caffeine reliably improves alertness and attention for a short time. Sleep, exercise, and treating things like sleep apnea or hearing loss have much stronger evidence than any pill.
===END===
