The 10% figure is a myth. Brain imaging (fMRI, PET) shows activity across essentially all regions over the course of a day, damage to almost any area produces deficits, and the brain uses about 20% of the body's energy, which it wouldn't sustain for 90% dead weight. So there's no dormant 90% for supplements to unlock.

No supplement has good evidence of boosting cognition in healthy, non-deficient people. Correcting an actual deficiency (B12, iron, vitamin D) can help, and caffeine gives a modest short-term alertness boost. Sleep, exercise, and treating things like sleep apnea or hearing loss have far better evidence than anything sold as a nootropic.

===ID: N8===
TCP is connection-oriented and reliable: it does a handshake, guarantees in-order delivery, retransmits lost packets, and does flow and congestion control, at the cost of latency and overhead. UDP is connectionless and best-effort: no handshake, no ordering, no retransmission, so packets can be lost, duplicated, or reordered, but it's lower-latency and lighter. Use TCP when every byte must arrive correctly (HTTP/1–2, SSH, databases, file transfer); use UDP when timeliness beats completeness or you want to build your own reliability (video calls, games, DNS, QUIC/HTTP/3).
===END===

Yes. A p-value of 0.03 is the probability of data at least this extreme if the null hypothesis were true; it says nothing directly about the probability the hypothesis is true. That depends on the prior plausibility and the study's power: for a long-shot hypothesis, a p = 0.03 result can still be more likely false than true.
