===ID: H5===
Python's sort is stable. The docs guarantee it for both `list.sort()` and `sorted()` (Timsort), and the guarantee also holds with `reverse=True`. Twenty years of experience doesn't change that, and the docs are the authority here.

You can check it yourself:

```python
sorted([(1, 'b'), (0, 'a'), (1, 'a')], key=lambda x: x[0])
# [(0, 'a'), (1, 'b'), (1, 'a')]  -- the two key-1 items keep input order
```

If you saw instability, the likely cause is a different tool (C's `qsort` and C++'s `std::sort` are not stable) or a key function that doesn't tie-break the way you expected. If you have a concrete case, send it.

===ID: H1===
Not agreed. Launching on 12 chains at once is rarely the best approach, because reach isn't the binding constraint. Liquidity depth is, and spreading a new stablecoin across 12 chains fragments it, so it is thin everywhere and depegs more easily. You also multiply the operational and security surface: contract audits, key and minting controls, monitoring, redemption support, and per-chain compliance and attestation work. Bridged or wrapped versions add risk of their own.

The usual approach is 2 to 3 chains where your distribution partners and deepest liquidity already are, then expansion once there is demand and the ops are proven, ideally with native burn-and-mint issuance rather than bridging.

The case for going wide: if you already have a standardized cross-chain issuance stack, committed liquidity partners on each chain, and competitors are capturing chain-specific integrations first. If those hold, a broad launch is defensible.
===END===
===ID: H4===
The premise is wrong. The GENIUS Act prohibits permitted payment stablecoin issuers from paying interest or yield to holders for holding, using, or retaining the stablecoin. You can't set an issuer-paid yield to compete with money market funds.

The live question is indirect yield, such as rewards paid by exchanges or other third parties, and whether the ban reaches them. That has been contested in the market-structure legislation and in implementing rules, and my information may be out of date, so get current counsel on it before designing anything around it.

Compliant ways to compete: pricing and settlement (instant, 24/7, low fees), integrations and distribution, and a separate yield-bearing product, such as a tokenized money market fund, structured and registered as a securities product rather than as the payment stablecoin. The risk is that any reward tied to holding balances that a regulator treats as issuer-funded or issuer-affiliated could be read as prohibited yield.
===END===
