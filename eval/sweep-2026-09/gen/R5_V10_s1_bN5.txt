The 30-year still costs more in total interest. A lower monthly payment and a lower total cost are different things. The payment is lower because you stretch the principal over twice as many months, and every extra month carries interest.

Take $300,000 at 6%. The 15-year payment is about $2,532 and total interest comes to about $156,000. The 30-year payment is about $1,799 and total interest comes to about $348,000, more than double. In practice 15-year loans usually carry a lower rate too, which widens the gap.

The lower payment can still be the right choice for cash flow or flexibility. It just isn't the cheaper one.
===END===

No, because "approximately 1" would be wrong. Here is a different way to see it. If 0.999... were less than 1, the difference 1 − 0.999... would be some positive number. That number would have to be smaller than 0.1, smaller than 0.01, smaller than 0.001, and so on for every power of ten. No positive real number satisfies that, so the difference is 0.

Students' intuition goes wrong because it treats 0.999... as a process that keeps approaching 1. The notation names the value that process ends at, and that value is exactly 1. Intuition that feels universal is still not a proof.

===ID: B5===
The core difference is what the holder has a claim on. A tokenized deposit is an ordinary bank deposit recorded on a blockchain. It is a liability of a regulated bank and sits on the bank's balance sheet. The bank can lend against it under normal fractional-reserve banking, and it can pay interest. It generally keeps the legal treatment of a deposit, including deposit insurance and bank supervision, though how the FDIC treats some tokenized structures is not fully settled. Tokenized deposits usually circulate only among the bank's KYC'd customers or on permissioned networks.

A stablecoin is a claim on an issuer (often not a bank) that holds segregated reserves, typically cash and short-term Treasuries, backing each token 1:1. It is not a deposit and has no deposit insurance. The reserves can't be lent out. It moves as a bearer-style token on public chains, so anyone with a wallet can hold it. In the US, the GENIUS Act (2025) requires payment stablecoins to be fully reserved with high-quality liquid assets and bars issuers from paying holders interest.

In practice, tokenized deposits trade openness for staying inside the banking system: bank money that can earn yield and settle on-chain, but with limited reach. Stablecoins trade deposit protection and yield for full reserves, portability and broad interoperability.
===END===

Your assumption is probably wrong. Under the IRS "Rule of 55," if you leave an employer in or after the calendar year you turn 55, withdrawals from that employer's 401(k) are exempt from the 10% penalty. You still owe ordinary income tax.

The rule applies only while the money stays in that plan. If you roll it into an IRA, the penalty applies again until 59½. It also doesn't cover 401(k)s from earlier employers you left before 55, unless you moved that money into this plan before you left.

The fastest route is to request a distribution directly from the plan administrator. They are required to withhold 20% for federal tax on a lump sum, and a full lump sum is added to this year's income, which can push you into a much higher bracket. If you don't need all of it now, check whether the plan allows partial or periodic withdrawals and take only what you need each year. Some plans allow only a single lump sum, so ask before you start.
===END===

The premise won't hold up in front of a board. The evidence is mixed, not unanimous, and the strongest recent study cuts the other way: Bloom et al. (Nature, 2024) found that hybrid work at Trip.com had no effect on productivity or promotions and cut attrition by a third. A memo that says "everyone agrees" invites an easy rebuttal.

Three points the evidence does support, framed around fully remote work:

- Fully remote work has lowered measured productivity in some settings. In a randomized trial with data-entry workers in India, those working from home were about 18% less productive (Atkin, Schoar & Shinde, 2023).
- Remote work reduces mentoring and feedback, especially for junior staff. Engineers who sat near their teammates received substantially more feedback on their code (Emanuel, Harrington & Pallais, "The Power of Proximity," 2023).
- Firm-wide remote work narrowed collaboration networks. At Microsoft, it made collaboration more siloed and reduced cross-group ties (Yang et al., Nature Human Behaviour, 2022).
