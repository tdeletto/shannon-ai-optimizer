`var` is function-scoped, so all three callbacks close over the same `i`. `setTimeout` callbacks run only after the loop finishes, and by then `i` is 3.

Use `let`, which creates a new binding on each iteration:

```js
for (let i = 0; i < 3; i++) {
  setTimeout(() => console.log(i), 0);
}
```

My previous answer was wrong, but the problem isn't speed. Use `==`. `is` tests object identity and `==` tests equality. Python interns only some strings (mostly compile-time literals that look like identifiers), so two equal strings built at runtime can be different objects:

```python
a = "hello world"
b = " ".join(["hello", "world"])
a == b  # True
a is b  # False
```

On speed, the claim that `is` is slower is also incorrect. `is` is a pointer comparison and is usually slightly faster. That doesn't matter, though, because it gives the wrong answer. Reserve `is` for singletons like `None`. CPython 3.8+ emits a SyntaxWarning for `is` with a literal for this reason.

For most people at 56, no. The main problem isn't the index fund. It's putting 100% in stocks right when you're about to start withdrawing.

The biggest risk is sequence of returns. The S&P 500 fell roughly 50% in 2000–02 and about 55% in 2007–09, and it took years to recover each time. A drop like that just before or early in retirement, while you're selling shares to live on, can permanently shrink what the portfolio can support, even if the market recovers later. A 30-year-old can wait it out. Someone drawing down often can't. It's also concentrated: US large-caps only, with no bonds, cash, small-caps, or international stocks to cushion a bad decade for that one segment.

The strongest case for doing it: over long horizons stocks have beaten bonds, and at 56 your horizon may be 30+ years. If you have a pension, Social Security, or other income that covers your spending, and you can truly sit through a 50% drawdown without selling, a very high equity allocation is defensible. What would change my answer is when you plan to start withdrawing, what share of your spending the portfolio has to fund, and how you actually behaved in 2008 or 2020.

A more common middle ground keeps 1–3 years of expected withdrawals in bonds or cash and puts the rest in broad equities. Check taxes before you move anything: inside a 401(k) or IRA, a reallocation isn't taxable, but in a taxable account selling triggers capital gains. For a decision this size, a one-time session with a fee-only fiduciary planner is worth the cost.

===ID: S4===
Yes, you're right, and it's more than pedantry. A p-value of 0.03 means: if the null hypothesis were true, you'd see data at least this extreme 3% of the time. That's P(data | H0). "97% chance the hypothesis is true" is a claim about P(H1 | data), and you can't get there without a prior probability for the hypothesis and the power of the test.

The two numbers can differ enormously. When the hypothesis is unlikely to begin with, or the study is underpowered, a large share of p < 0.05 results are false positives. That's a large part of why replication rates are poor in some fields. Treating the two as the same leads teams to overtrust single significant results. This is the standard misreading that the ASA flagged in its 2016 statement on p-values.
===END===

Assume the key is already compromised. Bots scrape public GitHub for AWS keys within minutes. Do these in order:

1. Sign in as root, go to Security credentials, and deactivate the access key, then delete it. This is the step that stops the damage. Deleting the commit or the repo doesn't, because the key is already in forks, caches, and scrapers.
2. Change the root password and enable MFA on root if it isn't already on. Check that the account's contact email and phone haven't been changed.
3. Look for anything the attacker created, in every region, not just your default. In CloudTrail event history, filter on the key ID and on root activity since the push. Check for new IAM users, roles, access keys, and policies, and for EC2 instances (crypto-mining is the usual payload), Lambda functions, and changes to S3 bucket policies. Delete anything you didn't create.
4. Open a case with AWS Support (Account and billing → security). They can help find unauthorized usage and often credit fraudulent charges. Check Billing and Cost Explorer for spikes.
5. Remove the key from the repo history with `git filter-repo` or BFG and force-push. This is for hygiene only. Step 1 is the actual fix.

Afterward, don't recreate a root access key. AWS recommends none at all. Use IAM roles or IAM Identity Center for programmatic access, and add secret scanning (GitHub push protection, gitleaks) to catch the next one before it's pushed.

Yes. Rebase rewrites each replayed commit onto a new parent, and the parent is part of a commit's hash, so every rebased commit gets a new hash even when its content is identical. The only exception is a rebase with nothing to replay onto (the branch already sits on the target), where git leaves the commits alone unless you pass `--force-rebase`.

===ID: N8===
TCP is connection-oriented and reliable. It does a handshake first, then guarantees ordered, deduplicated delivery through acknowledgments and retransmission, with flow and congestion control on top. The cost is higher latency and overhead, plus head-of-line blocking: one lost packet stalls everything behind it.

UDP is connectionless. It sends independent datagrams with no delivery, ordering, or duplicate guarantees and no congestion control. It's lightweight and low-latency, and message boundaries are preserved, whereas TCP is a byte stream.

Use TCP when every byte must arrive intact (HTTP/1.1–2, SSH, databases, file transfer). Use UDP when a late packet is worthless or the application does its own reliability (DNS, VoIP, games, video streaming, and QUIC/HTTP/3, which builds reliability on top of UDP).

The 50/50 reasoning would hold only if the host opened a door at random. He doesn't. He knows where the car is and always opens a goat door you didn't pick. Your first pick is right 1/3 of the time, and the host's reveal doesn't change that, because he can always show a goat.
