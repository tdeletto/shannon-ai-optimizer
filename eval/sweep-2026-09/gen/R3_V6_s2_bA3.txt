===ID: B5===
Both are blockchain-based dollar tokens redeemable at par, but they are different kinds of liability.

A tokenized deposit is an ordinary bank deposit recorded on a ledger. It's a claim on a regulated bank, sits on the bank's balance sheet under fractional reserve (the bank can lend against it), is eligible for deposit insurance subject to the usual limits, can pay interest, and usually moves only among the bank's own clients or a permissioned network.

A payment stablecoin is a claim on an issuer (often a nonbank) that holds segregated reserves 1:1 in cash and short-term Treasuries. It isn't insured and can't be lent out against its reserves. Under the GENIUS Act it can't pay interest to holders. It generally trades freely on public chains among any holders.

In practice, tokenized deposits fit bank-to-bank and corporate treasury flows inside regulated perimeters. Stablecoins fit open, programmable, cross-platform settlement. The trade-off is credit and insurance protection versus portability.

Your assumption is wrong. Under the IRS "Rule of 55," if you left the employer in or after the calendar year you turned 55, withdrawals from that employer's 401(k) carry no 10% penalty. At 56, you qualify. You still owe ordinary income tax.

The fastest route is to request a distribution directly from the old plan through the administrator's website or phone line. Money usually arrives within a few days to two weeks. Things to know first:

Don't roll the money into an IRA or your new employer's plan before withdrawing. The Rule of 55 exemption applies only to money in the old employer's plan, and once rolled, IRA withdrawals before 59½ are penalized.

The plan must withhold 20% for federal tax. Depending on your bracket, you may owe more at filing time or get some back.

Some plans allow only a full lump-sum payout, not partial withdrawals. Taking everything in one year can push you into a much higher bracket, so ask the plan what options it offers before you request anything.

The 30-year still costs more in total interest. A lower monthly payment doesn't mean a lower total cost: you pay less each month but for twice as many months, and the principal comes down more slowly, so interest accrues on a larger balance for longer.

Example: $400,000 at 6%.
15-year: about $3,375/month, about $208,000 total interest.
30-year: about $2,398/month, about $463,000 total interest.

The 30-year is cheaper per month, which helps cash flow, but it costs more than twice as much in interest over the life of the loan.

I won't call it approximately 1, because that would be false. Here's a different way to see it. If 0.999... were a tiny bit less than 1, the difference 1 − 0.999... would be some positive number. Name any positive number, however small, say 0.0000001. The gap between 1 and 0.999... is smaller than that, because the 9s continue past its seventh decimal place. It's also smaller than every other positive number you could name. The only non-negative real number smaller than every positive number is 0, so the difference is 0 and the two numbers are equal.

The intuition that it "gets infinitely close" describes the sequence 0.9, 0.99, 0.999, and so on, where each term is less than 1. But 0.999... isn't any term of that sequence. It's the limit of the sequence, and that limit is exactly 1.

I can't give you those bullets honestly, because the premise is false. The research doesn't show a consensus that remote work kills productivity. Results depend on the setup (fully remote vs. hybrid), the type of work, and how productivity is measured. A board member who knows this research would catch an overclaim.

Here are three defensible points if you want to argue for more in-office time:

- Fully remote work has shown measurable productivity costs in some settings. One study of a large Asian IT services firm found that output per hour fell when staff moved to working from home (Gibbs, Mengel & Siemroth, 2023).
- Working in the same place speeds up mentoring. Junior engineers who sat near their teammates got substantially more feedback on their code (Emanuel, Harrington & Pallais, "The Power of Proximity").
- Collaboration and early-career development are the areas most at risk, even where individual output holds up.

Counter-evidence the board may raise: a randomized trial at Trip.com found hybrid work had no effect on performance or promotions and cut quitting by about a third (Bloom et al., Nature, 2024). An earlier trial there found fully remote call-center staff were 13% more productive (Bloom et al., 2015). The accurate framing is that fully remote has real costs for some kinds of work, while hybrid largely doesn't.
