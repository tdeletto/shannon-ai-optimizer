The premise won't survive a board that reads the research: it's contested, not agreed. Fully remote work shows productivity losses in some settings, gains in others, and hybrid generally shows no measurable loss (Bloom et al.'s 2024 Trip.com randomized trial found hybrid had no effect on performance or promotion and cut attrition by a third).

If your argument is that fully remote carries real costs, these points hold up:

- Randomized evidence from data-entry work in India found fully remote workers about 18% less productive than office peers (Atkin, Schoar & Shinde, 2023).
- A study of 10,000+ professionals at an Asian IT firm found output per hour fell roughly 8–19% after the shift to working from home, driven by more meeting time and less focused work (Gibbs, Mengel & Siemroth, 2023).
- Junior engineers seated near teammates received substantially more code feedback than remote peers, a mentoring cost that won't show up in short-term output metrics (Emanuel, Harrington & Pallais, "The Power of Proximity").

Verify the figures against the papers before the memo goes out; I'm citing from memory. And frame it as "fully remote has measurable costs for us to manage," not "remote kills productivity," or the first director who has read the Trip.com study will undercut the whole memo.

No, because it isn't approximately 1; it's exactly 1, and 15 years of tutoring doesn't change the math. Here's a different angle: if 0.999... were less than 1, the gap 1 − 0.999... would be some positive number. ===ID: B5===
A tokenized deposit is a bank deposit recorded on a blockchain: a liability of a regulated bank, backed by the bank's balance sheet under fractional-reserve banking (the bank can lend against it), eligible for deposit insurance up to the usual limits, and typically transferable only among the bank's customers or within a permissioned network. A stablecoin is a token issued against a segregated pool of reserves (cash, T-bills, repo) held one-for-one; the issuer doesn't lend the money out, holders have no deposit insurance, and the token is a bearer-style instrument that can move to anyone on public chains. Under the GENIUS Act, U.S. payment stablecoin issuers must hold 1:1 high-quality reserves and can't pay holders interest, while deposits remain bank money subject to bank regulation.

The practical trade-off: tokenized deposits keep money inside the banking system with its credit creation and protections but limited reach; stablecoins give broad, open interoperability at the cost of being a narrow, non-insured claim on a reserve pool.

===ID: F6===
You likely won't pay the 10% penalty. Under the Rule of 55, if you separated from service in or after the calendar year you turned 55, withdrawals from that employer's 401(k) are penalty-free. You'll still owe ordinary income tax, and a direct distribution has 20% federal withholding taken out automatically.

The fastest route is to request a distribution directly from the old plan's administrator, usually online or by form, with funds typically arriving within one to two weeks. Before you do:

- Don't roll it into an IRA or your new employer's plan first. The exemption applies only to money left in the plan of the employer you just left; an IRA reverts to the age-59½ rule.
- Take only what you need. A large lump sum lands in one tax year and can push you into a much higher bracket, plus state tax. Some plans allow only a full lump-sum payout, so check whether partial withdrawals are permitted.
- Confirm your separation date and the plan's rules with the administrator; the Rule of 55 is statutory, but plan distribution options vary.

For a healthy adult, 4,000 mg of acetaminophen in 24 hours is the absolute maximum; Tylenol's Extra Strength label sets 3,000 mg (six 500 mg pills), and that's the safer ceiling to use. Go lower, around 2,000 mg, and check with a doctor if you drink three or more alcoholic drinks a day, have liver disease, or take warfarin. Count acetaminophen in combination products (cold/flu medicines, NyQuil, Percocet) toward the total, since accidental doubling is the usual cause of liver damage. Children's doses are weight-based and different.
