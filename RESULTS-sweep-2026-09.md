# Judge sweep, September 2026: what v9.0 is adopted on

A separate Claude session (2026-09-27 to 09-30) ran an eleven-arm search over contract wordings, scored by blind LLM judges against per-item rubrics. Its best arm, V8, is the v9.0 contract. The complete archive (items, rubrics, every arm's text, every generation, every judge score, the analysis scripts) is in `eval/sweep-2026-09/`, and every number below reproduces from it:

```
cd eval/sweep-2026-09 && python3 items.py
python3 analyze.py R4 V0      # any round: R1-R5 (dev), HO / HS (held-out)
python3 pair.py               # arm-vs-arm ablation pairs
python3 cluster.py            # the same comparisons with the generation batch as the unit
```

`V0` is v8.2 byte-for-byte. `V8` is v9.0 byte-for-byte.

## Design

50 items across 15 categories (sycophancy, user-is-right, false premise, pushback hold/fold, same-answer, brevity, brevity-vs-caveat, uncertainty, staleness, recommendation, code, ask-vs-act, agentic report, procedural/urgent), each with a rubric describing the ideal answer and its hard failures. 38 were the dev set, used to choose between arms over five rounds; 12 were held out and scored once, at the end, on the chosen arm only. Each arm answered every item three times. Two judges, Opus and Sonnet, scored every answer blind with labels shuffled per item: correctness 1–5, concision 1–5, overall 1–10, hard-failure Y/N. The search stopped after two rounds without a significant gain.

## Results, overall score out of 10

| set | v8.2 | v9.0 | difference, 95% CI (items) | items won/lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dev, round 4 | 7.91 | 8.40 | +0.50 [+0.27, +0.72] | 24 / 3 |
| dev, round 5 | 8.06 | 8.58 | +0.52 [+0.30, +0.75] | 19 / 4 |
| held-out, generated by Opus | 8.25 | 8.97 | +0.72 [+0.37, +1.08] | 9 / 1 |
| held-out, generated by Sonnet | 8.41 | 8.74 | +0.33 [−0.06, +0.73] | 6 / 3 |

For scale: no system prompt scored 6.54 against v8.2's 8.16 in round 1 (−1.61 [−1.99, −1.23], 0 items won of 30). v8.2 was already doing most of the work.

The gain is concision, not correctness. Mean correctness moved 4.94 → 4.94 (round 4) and 4.89 → 4.96 (held-out Opus); concision moved 3.86 → 4.47 and 4.11 → 4.79. Hard-failure flags stayed between 0% and 0.9% of judgments for every contract arm in every round (no system prompt: 1.7%). Answers ran 10–17% shorter in words (round 4: 130 → 108 mean; held-out Opus: 111 → 95).

Hedge and format-marker rates, scored with `shannon_eval.HEDGES` and `FORMAT_MARK`, did not regress: hedges 0.12 → 0.08 per 100 words in round 4, 0.17 → 0.09 in round 5; format markers 0.48 → 0.36 and 0.61 → 0.40. This matters because the August external rewrite, which also added exemplars, was rejected on exactly that axis (hedges +0.26 per 100 words). v9.0 keeps the explicit hedge list that rewrite dropped. Hedge counts are small in absolute terms (about ten per arm per round), so read these as "no regression", not as an improvement.

## The ablation: what earns the words

Four arms isolate the parts of v9.0.

| arm | contents | words | vs v8.2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| V5 | every v9.0 rule change, **no** calibration examples | 856 | +0.03 [−0.13, +0.20] (round 3) |
| V6 | V5 plus four of the six examples | 970 | +0.38 [+0.18, +0.60] (round 3), +0.47 (round 4) |
| V7 | v8.2 plus four examples and one ask-before-irreversible line, **none** of the other new rules | 767 | +0.46 [+0.25, +0.69] (round 4) |
| V8 = v9.0 | V5 plus all six examples | 1,035 | +0.50 (round 4), +0.52 (round 5) |

Head to head in round 4: V8 − V7 = +0.04 [−0.13, +0.20]; V8 − V6 = +0.03; V7 − V6 = −0.01.

The calibration examples carry the gain. The rule changes without them (V5) do nothing measurable; the examples without most of the rule changes (V7) do everything V8 does. **About 270 of v9.0's 1,035 words have no measured effect.** They are shipped because V8 is the text that was held-out tested and V7 is not, and that trade is stated under *Decision* below rather than hidden.

Two further arms, round 5: V9 (V7 plus the last two examples and a "verify your evidence before calling a check proof" line) scored V8 − V9 = +0.11 [−0.04, +0.25]; V10 (V8 plus that same verify sentence appended to "Report state") scored V8 − V10 = +0.34 [+0.15, +0.57]. One sentence costing a third of a point is either a real harm or a measure of how noisy arm-level differences are in this design; the second reading is at least as likely (see below). Either way it was not adopted.

## Re-analysis: the batch, not the item, is the unit

The archived CIs bootstrap over items. But each generation call answered a whole batch of conversations (19 dev items, or all 12 held-out items) in one pass, so answers within a batch share whatever that call's sampling did, and an item-level bootstrap understates the variance. `cluster.py` re-tests with the batch as the unit.

| comparison | batch units | mean | t |
|---|---|---|---|
| v9.0 − v8.2, round 4 | 6 | +0.50 | +3.98 |
| v9.0 − v8.2, round 5 | 6 | +0.52 | +4.05 |
| v9.0 − v8.2, held-out Opus | 3 | +0.72 | +2.43 |
| v9.0 − v8.2, held-out Sonnet | 3 | +0.17 | +0.91 |
| V8 − V7, round 4 | 6 | +0.04 | +0.65 |
| V8 − V9, round 5 | 6 | +0.11 | +0.99 |
| V8 − V10, round 5 | 6 | +0.28 | +2.70 |

Pooled, v9.0 beats v8.2 in **17 of 18** batches (sign test p ≈ 0.0001). The headline survives. The held-out Sonnet result does not reach significance at either unit, and nothing below the examples-vs-no-examples split does either.

## What this does not establish

- **Not the deployment path.** Generators were subagents role-playing the assistant, with the contract pasted into the prompt inside `<user_preferences>` and several conversations answered per call. That approximates the claude.ai preferences field; it is not a system prompt, a Project, or one conversation per call.
- **Same-family judges with an aligned brief.** Both judges are Claude models, and their standing brief (`GOALS` in `harness.py`) restates the contract's own priorities: answer first, prose over bullets, no headers in short answers. Both arms carry those rules, so the comparison is fair between them, but the absolute concision scores partly measure compliance with Shannon's style. Correctness, the axis least exposed to that, was flat.
- **Same author for rubrics and arms.** The session that wrote the variants wrote the rubrics.
- **Twelve held-out items,** one held-out pass per generator model. The Sonnet arm points the right way and is not significant.
- **Model versions unrecorded.** The archive says "Opus" and "Sonnet" without IDs.
- **`shannon_eval.py` was not run on v9.0.** The repo's programmatic probes (stance-flip, citation rebuttal, `user_is_right`, substance completeness) have not seen this text. The coverage test's rule-phrase checks pass; the probes' behaviour is unmeasured.
- **Tokens not counted.** No tokenizer was available. ~1,520 is an estimate from v8.2's 4.2 characters per token; v8.2 was 941.
- **One failure no arm fixed.** In about 1 run in 12, every arm, v8.2 included, presented a check as proof of something it had not tested.

## Decision

v9.0 ships V8. The gain over v8.2 replicated in two dev rounds and one of two held-out sets, survived the batch-level re-test, and arrived without a hedge or formatting regression, at the cost of ~580 more tokens per turn.

The strongest case against: on this sweep's own evidence, V7 (767 words, ~1,150 tokens estimated) is as good, and the repo's seventh principle says rules that can't be shown to do anything come out. V8 is shipped over V7 only because V8 is held-out tested and V7 is not. **A held-out run of V7 against V8 that ties should replace v9.0 with V7.** That arm is preserved as `variants/v9.0-ablation-examples-only.md`; v8.2 is preserved as `variants/v8.2-contract.md`.
